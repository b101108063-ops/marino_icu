## Chapter 20: Acute Coronary Syndromes

The study of the causes of things must be preceded by
the study of things caused.

John Hughlings Jackson (1835–1911)

One of the seminal moments in cardiac critical care was the discovery (in 1980) that transmural
myocardial infarction was the result of an occlusive thrombus in a coronary artery. Advances in
reperfusion therapy have contributed to 20% decline in annual mortality rate from coronary disease over
the past decade. However, coronary artery disease continues to be the leading cause of death in the
United States.

This chapter describes the diagnosis and early management of acute myocardial infarction and
unstable angina (the acute coronary syndromes), with an emphasis on management in the first 24–36
hours. Also included is a section on acute aortic dissection.

### 導讀摘要

- **急性冠心症分類**：STEMI（ST elevation MI，完整冠脈阻塞）、NSTEMI（非ST elevation MI，部分阻塞）、Unstable Angina（反覆開關性阻塞）
- **心肌梗塞診斷**：症狀 + 12導程ECG（10分鐘內）+ 高敏感性Troponin（hs-cTn，3小時後可測得）；hs-cTn 1小時內上升 >10% 表示急性缺血
- **再灌注治療**：STEMI 首選 PCI（90分鐘內）；無法執行時使用血栓溶解劑（Alteplase、Reteplase、Tenecteplase）
- **抗血栓治療**：Aspirin（162–325 mg負載劑量）+ P2Y12抑制劑（Clopidogrel、Ticagrelor、Prasugrel）；必要時加 GP IIb/IIIa抑制劑
- **主動脈剝離**：血壓管理目標是收縮壓 **100–120 mmHg** 或 MAP **60–70 mmHg**；首選 β-blocker（Esmolol、Labetalol）降低 dP/dt

### Coronary Thrombosis

Pathogenesis
Acute myocardial infarction results from an occlusive thrombus formed after rupture of an atherosclerotic
plaque, releasing thrombogenic lipids. Inflammation plays a major role in plaque rupture.

Role of Oxidative Injury
Myeloperoxidase (MPO), released by leukocytes, generates hypochlorite and oxidizes lipoproteins in
atherosclerotic plaques. Plasma MPO levels correlate with presence and severity of CAD.

Clinical Syndromes

**Table 20.1**
*ECG Changes in Acute Coronary Syndromes*

| Condition | Pattern | Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| **STEMI** | ST Elevation | ≥1.5 mm (♀) or ≥2.0 mm (♂) in V2–V₃; ≥1 mm in other leads |
| Posterior STEMI | ST Depression V1–V3 + Positive T wave | ST depression with upright T in V1–V₃ |
| NSTE-ACS | ST Depression ≥0.5 mm | Horizontal or downsloping ST depression; T wave inversion >1 mm in ≥5 leads |

**Table 20.2**
*Thrombolytic Therapy for Acute Coronary Occlusion*

| Agent | Dosing Regimen | Patency Rate at 90 min |
|---|---|---|
| Alteplase (tPA) | 15 mg IV bolus, then 0.75 mg/kg over 30 min, then 0.5 mg/kg over 60 min (max 100 mg total) | 73–84% |
| Reteplase (rPA) | 10 Units IV bolus, repeat in 30 min | 84% |
| Tenecteplase (TNK-tPA) | Single IV bolus: 30–50 mg based on weight | 85% |

### Diagnostic Evaluation

**Clinical Presentation**:
- 80% have chest discomfort (pain, pressure, tightness)
- Abrupt onset lasting >15 minutes
- Can involve shoulders, neck, arms, abdomen
- Nausea, vomiting, dyspnea common

**Electrocardiogram**: Obtain within **10 minutes** of first patient contact

**Troponin Assay**:
- High-sensitivity assay (hs-cTn) can detect elevations as early as **3 hours** after symptom onset
- Myocardial necrosis likely: initial hs-cTn elevated AND ≥3 hours since symptom onset
- Significant change (>10%) in second hs-cTn = acute ischemia

### Cardioprotective Measures

**Table 20.3**
*Cardioprotective Measures*

| Agent | Dosing Regimens and Comments |
|---|---|
| **Oxygen** | Maintain SaO₂ ≥90%. Use judiciously — promotes coronary vasoconstriction. |
| **Nitroglycerin** | Chest pain: 0.4 mg sublingual/spray q5min × 3. Recurrent pain/high BP/CHF: infuse 5 µg/min initially, titrate. Avoid in RV infarction, aortic stenosis, within 24–48 hrs of phosphodiesterase inhibitors. |
| **Morphine** | 4–8 mg IV, then 2–8 mg IV q5–15min PRN. May reduce antiplatelet effect of P2Y12 inhibitors. |
| **Metoprolol** | 5 mg IV bolus, repeat q5min × 2 if tolerated. Caution in acute HF, bradycardia, heart block. |

### Antiplatelet Measures

**Table 20.4**
*Antiplatelet Measures for Acute Coronary Thrombosis*

| Agent | Dosing Regimen |
|---|---|
| **Aspirin** | 162–325 mg chewable at first contact, then 81 mg/day (or 325 mg/day). Use clopidogrel if aspirin allergy. |
| **Clopidogrel** (P2Y12) | 300–600 mg loading dose, then 75 mg/day |
| **Ticagrelor** (P2Y12) | 180 mg loading dose, then 90 mg BID |
| **Prasugrel** (P2Y12) | 60 mg loading dose, then 10 mg/day |
| **Eptifibatide** (GP IIb-IIIa) | 180 µg/kg bolus, then 2 µg/kg/min (reduce 50% if CrCL <50 mL/min) |
| **Tirofiban** (GP IIb-IIIa) | 25 µg/kg bolus, then 0.15 µg/kg/min (reduce 50% if CrCL <30 mL/min) |

### Acute Aortic Dissection

Acute aortic dissection requires blood pressure management to reduce aortic wall stress (dP/dt).

**Antihypertensive Therapy**:

**Table 20.5**
*Antihypertensive Therapy for Acute Aortic Dissection*

| Drug | Dosing Regimens and Comments |
|---|---|
| **Esmolol** | 500 µg/kg IV bolus, then 50 µg/kg/min infusion; increase by 25 µg/kg/min to desired BP or max 200 µg/kg/min. Give repeat bolus before each increment. Ultra rapid-acting β-blocker; avoid in acute HF. |
| **Labetalol** | 20 mg IV over 2 min, then 20–40 mg IV q10min PRN, or infuse 1–2 mg/min and titrate. Combined α- and β-blocker; use as monotherapy. Avoid in acute HF. |
| **Metoprolol** | 5 mg IV bolus, repeat q5min × 2 if tolerated. Caution in acute HF, bradycardia, heart block. |

**Goals**: Systolic BP **100–120 mmHg** or MAP **60–70 mmHg**
