## Chapter 53: Nonpharmaceutical Poisons

> "It is astonishing with how little reading a doctor can practice medicine, but it is not astonishing how badly he may do it."
> — Sir William Osler

This chapter describes the toxic syndromes produced by exposure to nonpharmaceutical poisons, including carbon monoxide, cyanide, and the toxic alcohols (methanol and ethylene glycol). They are less common than drug overdoses but can be lethal if not recognized.

---

## Introduction

## 導讀摘要

- **一氧化碳（CO）中毒**：CO對血紅素的親和力為氧氣的 **200倍以上**；臨床表現多樣，**COHb數值與臨床嚴重度相關性差**；**脈搏血氧儀無法區分COHb與oxyhemoglobin**（顯示正常值），需使用 **CO-oximetry** 確診。
- **CO治療**：100%氧氣吸入（COHb半衰期： room air 320 min → 100% O₂ 74 min）；高流量、加热、加濕鼻導管（最高40 L/min）；高壓氧療效證據不足。
- **氰化物中毒**：源於煙霧吸入（含CO及HCN）、口服氰化物鹽、**sodium nitroprusside**輸注；抑制**cytochrome oxidase**，阻斷細胞氧化磷酸化，導致**乳酸性酸中毒**；血漿乳酸常 **>10 mmol/L**；首選解毒劑：**Hydroxocobalamin**（5 g IV，cardia arrest時10 g）。
- **甲醇/乙二醇中毒**：皆由**alcohol dehydrogenase**代謝；乙二醇最終產物為**草酸**（與鈣形成不溶性草酸鈣結晶，沉積於腎小管→急性腎損傷）；甲醇最終產物為**蟻酸**（破壞視網膜/視神經→視力障礙為特徵）。
- **解毒療程**：首選 **Fomepizole**（15 mg/kg loading，10 mg/kg q12h維持）；血漿濃度 **>20 mg/dL** 或嚴重酸血症（pH <7.1）、顯著器官損傷時需**血液透析**；乙二醇中毒另加 **thiamine** 及 **pyridoxine**；甲醇中毒改用 **Folinic acid**（而非thiamine/pyridoxine）。

Carbon monoxide (CO) is produced by the incomplete combustion of organic matter. The principal sources are smoke inhalation from house fires, poorly functioning heating systems, and automobile exhaust. Catalytic converters have reduced CO emissions from automobiles by more than 95%.

### Pathophysiology

CO binds to heme moieties in hemoglobin (at the same site that binds oxygen) to produce carboxyhemoglobin (COHb). The affinity of CO for hemoglobin is over 200 times greater than that of oxygen, and CO pressures of only 0.4 mm Hg can fully saturate hemoglobin.

**Effects on oxygenation:**

- CO reduces arterial O₂ content proportionally to COHb level
- CO shifts the oxyhemoglobin dissociation curve to the left, impairing oxygen unloading at tissues
- CO inhibits cytochrome oxidase (impairing mitochondrial ATP production)
- CO activates neutrophils, promoting oxidative injury

Importantly, there is a **poor correlation between COHb levels and clinical severity**.

### Clinical Features

- **Early:** Headache (usually frontal), dizziness, drowsiness, nausea, vomiting, ataxia, agitation, confusion, delirium
- **Severe:** Obtundation or coma, generalized seizures, respiratory failure, hemodynamic instability, lactic acidosis
- **Cherry-red skin color:** Classically described but a rare occurrence

### Delayed Neurologic Sequelae

One to four weeks after acute CO poisoning, a neuropsychiatric syndrome can emerge, including mental deterioration (impaired judgment, memory loss, cognitive defects) and parkinsonism. The correlation with COHb levels has been difficult to establish.

### Diagnosis

**Pulse oximetry is NOT reliable** — it cannot distinguish COHb from oxyhemoglobin, giving spuriously normal readings.

**CO-oximetry** (8-wavelength oximeter) is required to measure COHb levels:

- Healthy nonsmokers: <1% COHb
- Smokers: 3–5% or higher
- Threshold for elevated COHb: 3–4% (nonsmokers), 10% (smokers)

### Management

**100% oxygen inhalation:**

- The elimination half-life of COHb is 320 minutes on room air and 74 minutes on 100% O₂
- Less than 1½ hours on 100% O₂ should reduce COHb to normal
- Use high-flow, heated, humidified nasal O₂ (up to 40 L/min) to ensure 100% O₂ delivery

**Hyperbaric oxygen:** Evidence is inconclusive; no convincing superiority over normobaric O₂ therapy.

**Cardiac concerns:** Myocardial injury occurs in ~40% of moderate to severe CO poisoning. Obtain ECG and cardiac biomarkers in all such patients.

## Cyanide Poisoning

Cyanide is a lethal toxin. Sources include:

- **Inhalation** of hydrogen cyanide gas during house fires (house fires generate both CO and HCN)
- **Oral ingestion** of potassium or sodium cyanide (converted to HCN by gastric acidity)
- **Iatrogenic** from sodium nitroprusside infusions (contains cyanide moieties)

### Pathogenesis

Cyanide ions have high affinity for metalloproteins. The primary target is **cytochrome oxidase** in the mitochondrial electron-transport chain. Cyanide inhibition of cytochrome oxidase halts oxidative energy production, causing:

- Impeded pyruvate uptake into mitochondria
- Increased lactate production and metabolic (lactic) acidosis
- Rapid progression to fatality if untreated

### Clinical Features

- **Early:** Agitation, tachycardia, hypertension, tachypnea (compensatory response to metabolic acidosis)
- **Progression:** Loss of consciousness, bradycardia, hypotension, cardiac arrest
- **Labs:** Plasma lactate often >10 mmol/L; venous blood may appear "arterialized" (high PO₂ due to lack of tissue O₂ utilization)
- **After smoke inhalation:** Time from symptom onset to cardiac arrest can be <5 minutes
- **After oral ingestion:** Progression may be slower (minutes to hours)

### Diagnosis

Cyanide poisoning is a clinical diagnosis. Whole blood cyanide levels are not immediately available. Suspect cyanide poisoning in smoke inhalation victims who are hemodynamically unstable or have plasma lactate ≥10 mmol/L.

### Treatment

**TABLE 53.1 — Cyanide Antidotes**

| Antidote | Dosing | Comments |
|---|---|---|
| **Hydroxocobalamin** | 5 g IV over 15 min; 10 g for cardiac arrest | **Antidote of choice**; forms cyanocobalamin (excreted in urine); safe; may cause transient BP elevation and reddish urine |
| **Sodium thiosulfate** | 12.5 g IV in 50 mL sterile water | Converts cyanide to thiocyanate (renally excreted); use with hydroxocobalamin; **avoid in renal failure** (thiocyanate is neurotoxic) |
| **Amyl nitrite (inhalant)** | Inhale 15 sec, rest 15 sec; repeat with fresh ampule every 3 min | Temporary measure when IV unavailable; **contraindicated in smoke inhalation** (promotes methemoglobin formation) |

> **Empiric treatment is recommended for smoke inhalation victims** who are hemodynamically unstable or have lactate ≥10 mmol/L.

## Toxic Alcohols

Ethylene glycol and methanol are common household, automotive, and industrial products. They produce toxic syndromes characterized by metabolic acidosis.

---

### Ethylene Glycol

Ethylene glycol is the main ingredient in automotive antifreeze and deicer products. It has a sweet taste, making it a method of attempted suicide.

#### Pathogenesis

Ethylene glycol is readily absorbed from the GI tract; 80% of the ingested dose is metabolized in the liver via alcohol dehydrogenase and lactate dehydrogenase, ending in **oxalic acid** formation. Key features:

- Each reaction converts NAD to NADH, promoting pyruvate→lactate conversion (elevated lactate)
- Intermediate acids contribute to metabolic acidosis with elevated anion gap
- Oxalic acid combines with calcium to form **insoluble calcium oxalate crystals** that precipitate in tissues (especially renal tubules), causing renal tubular injury

#### Clinical Features

- **Early:** Nausea, vomiting, apparent inebriation (no odor of alcohol on breath)
- **Severe:** Depressed consciousness, coma, generalized seizures, renal failure (can be a late finding, 24 hours after ingestion), pulmonary edema, cardiovascular collapse

#### Laboratory Findings

- Metabolic acidosis with elevated anion gap (glycolic acid and oxalic acid)
- Elevated osmolal gap
- Elevated serum lactate (can be misleading — high anion gap may be mistakenly attributed to lactate)
- **Calcium oxalate crystalluria** (~50% of cases): Thin, monohydrate crystals are more characteristic than box-shaped dihydrate crystals

#### Plasma Levels

- Ethylene glycol level >20 mg/dL is toxic
- Glycolic acid level >8 mmol/L has been proposed as an indication for hemodialysis

#### Treatment

**TABLE 53.2 — Fomepizole Dosing for Toxic Alcohol Poisoning**

| Step | Dosing |
|---|---|
| Loading dose | 15 mg/kg IV |
| Maintenance | 10 mg/kg IV every 12 hours for 4 doses |
| If level >20 mg/dL after 4 doses | Increase to 15 mg/kg every 12 hours |
| With hemodialysis | 15 mg/kg every 4 hours |
| Continue until | Toxic alcohol level <20 mg/dL; pH normal; patient asymptomatic |

**Hemodialysis:** Indicated for severe acidemia (pH <7.1), evidence of significant end-organ damage (coma, seizures, renal insufficiency). Multiple sessions may be needed.

**Adjuncts:** Thiamine 100 mg IV daily and pyridoxine 100 mg IV daily (divert glyoxylic acid to nontoxic metabolites).

---

### Methanol

Methanol (wood alcohol) is found in shellac, varnish, paint remover, windshield washer fluid, and solid cooking fuel (e.g., Sterno®).

#### Pathogenesis

Methanol is metabolized by alcohol dehydrogenase to **formic acid**, which:

- Dissociates readily, producing metabolic acidosis with high anion gap
- Inhibits cytochrome oxidase, blocking oxidative energy production
- Particularly damages the retina, optic nerve, and basal ganglia

#### Clinical Features

- **Early (within 6 hours):** Apparent inebriation without ethanol odor
- **Later (6–24 hours):** Visual disturbances (scotoma, blurred vision, complete blindness), depressed consciousness, coma, generalized seizures
- **Retinal examination:** Papilledema and generalized retinal edema
- Visual disturbances are **characteristic of methanol poisoning** (not seen in ethylene glycol)

#### Laboratory Findings

- Metabolic acidosis, high anion gap, high osmolal gap
- **No crystalluria** (unlike ethylene glycol)
- Plasma methanol assay: Level >20 mg/dL is toxic

#### Treatment

Same as ethylene glycol poisoning, with the following differences:

1. **Visual impairment** is an indication for dialysis in methanol poisoning
2. **Folinic acid** (leucovorin) is used instead of thiamine/pyridoxine: 1 mg/kg IV (max 50 mg) every 4 hours. Use folic acid if folinic acid is unavailable.

---

## A Final Word

Key points from this chapter:

1. **Carbon monoxide poisoning can be missed** if you rely on pulse oximetry. CO-oximetry is required to detect elevated COHb.

2. **Smoke inhalation victims** with severe lactic acidosis or hemodynamic instability should be treated empirically for cyanide poisoning.

3. **Methanol and ethylene glycol poisoning** should be considered in any patient with a high-anion-gap metabolic acidosis of unclear etiology.

4. **Calcium oxalate monohydrate crystals** in urine support the diagnosis of ethylene glycol poisoning.
