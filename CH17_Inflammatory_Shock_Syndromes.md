## Chapter 17: Inflammatory Shock Syndromes

Inflammation is not itself considered a disease but a salutary
operation… but when it cannot accomplish that salutary purpose… it
does mischief.

John Hunter, MD (a)

The introductory quote is from an 18th-Century British surgeon who noted that inflammation has two
opposing "faces": it normally acts to promote healing, but it can also become a source of tissue injury.
This chapter describes three life-threatening conditions where inflammation plays a major role: septic shock,
toxic shock, and anaphylactic shock.

### 導讀摘要

- **敗血性休克**：美國住院死亡主因；美國死亡率約 30–50%； mortality 與病原菌無關，與宿主反應有關
- **敗血性休克的病理機制**：不是組織缺氧，而是**粒線體功能障礙（mitochondrial dysfunction）**； ScvO2 會誤導（因 O2 extraction 缺陷）
- **敗血性休克治療**：晶體溶液 **30 mL/kg**（3小時內）；首選血管加壓劑是 **norepinephrine**；目標 MAP ≥65 mmHg
- **TSS（Toxic Shock Syndrome）**：Staph TSS 死亡率 <2–6%；Strep TSS 死亡率 **35%**；Clindamycin 可抑制毒素產生
- **過敏性反應（Anaphylaxis）**：首選治療是 **Epinephrine**（0.3–0.5 mg IM）；Corticosteroids 無急性效益

### Inflammatory Injury

The inflammatory response is designed to eliminate external threats and heal damaged tissues. However,
severe or persistent inflammation can produce widespread tissue injury that can become self-sustaining.

Neutrophil Activation
In the initial stage, circulating neutrophils experience a precipitous rise in cellular O2 consumption
(up to **20-fold**) for 15–20 minutes. This is the "respiratory burst," which generates reactive oxygen
species (ROS) including superoxide radical, hydrogen peroxide, and hydroxyl radical.

Oxidant Injury
ROS can damage all vital cell components: membrane lipids, proteins, and DNA. Oxidant tissue injury
occurs when ROS activity exceeds protective antioxidants (oxidant stress).

Endothelial Dysfunction
Inflammation promotes endothelial dysfunction via oxidant stress, reducing nitric oxide levels:
O₂⁻ + NO⁻ → ONOO⁻ (peroxynitrite)

Inflammatory Organ Injury

|---|---|

### SEPTIC SHOCK

Sepsis is responsible for **1 in 2 to 3 in-hospital deaths** in the US (16) and **1 in 5 deaths** worldwide (17).
Mortality: about **30%** when present on admission, **50%** when developing after admission (18).

Definitions (Sepsis-3, 2016)
1. **Sepsis**: Infection (suspected/confirmed) associated with life-threatening organ dysfunction
2. **Septic shock**: Sepsis with vasopressor requirement AND lactate >**2 mmol/L**

Pathophysiology
The hemodynamic aberrations in septic shock:
- **Principal problem**: Hypotension from systemic vasodilation (↑ nitric oxide)
- **Oxidant injury** → fluid extravasation → hypovolemia
- **Proinflammatory cytokines** → cardiac dysfunction (but CO usually ↑ initially due to tachycardia and ↓ LV afterload)
- **Splanchnic blood flow reduced** despite increased CO

**Mitochondrial Dysfunction**: Organ dysfunction in sepsis is attributed to defective high-energy ATP
production in mitochondria, NOT inadequate tissue oxygenation.

Monitoring
1. **ScvO2 may be falsely elevated** due to ↓ O2 extraction in sepsis
2. **PCO2 gap** is useful for identifying tissue hypoperfusion (not affected by O2 extraction defects)
3. **Lactate** has prognostic significance; normalization is an important goal

Management

**Table 17.2**
*The Initial Management of Septic Shock*

| Intervention | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Volume Infusion** | 30 mL/kg crystalloid within 3 hours of presentation |
| **Vasopressor** | Start norepinephrine; titrate to MAP ≥65 mmHg |
| **Vasopressin** | Add if norepinephrine 0.25–0.5 μg/kg/min is insufficient |
| **Epinephrine** | Add as 3rd vasopressor if refractory |
| **Corticosteroids** | IV hydrocortisone 50 mg q6h if norepinephrine >0.25 μg/kg/min |
| **Antibiotics** | Within 1 hour of presentation; obtain cultures first |

Volume Resuscitation
After initial resuscitation, adjust daily fluid infusion to match fluid losses. Positive fluid balance
increases mortality (33,34).

Vasopressors
**Norepinephrine** is first-line. Note: norepinephrine has immunosuppressant effects (36).

Corticosteroids
Recommended for patients requiring norepinephrine ≥**0.25 μg/kg/min**. Regimen: **hydrocortisone 50 mg IV q6h**.
Does NOT improve survival but may shorten shock duration by ~1.5 days.

Antimicrobial Therapy
Start within **1 hour**. Obtain blood cultures (≥2 sets, ≥20 mL each) first. Empiric coverage should be
broad-spectrum.

### TOXIC SHOCK SYNDROME

Toxic shock syndrome (TSS) is caused by toxin-producing **Staphylococcus aureus** and **Streptococcus
pyogenes** (Group A strep). Characterized by rapidly progressive multiorgan failure and DIC.

**Table 17.3**
*Comparative Features of the Toxic Shock Syndromes*

| Feature | Staphylococcal TSS | Streptococcal TSS |
|---|---|---|
| Notable sources | Menstruation (tampons), surgical wounds, 35% unknown | Necrotizing fasciitis, postpartum sepsis, 45% unknown |
| Positive blood cultures | <5% | 60% |
| Antibiotics | MSSA: cefazolin + clindamycin; MRSA: vancomycin + clindamycin | High-dose penicillin + clindamycin |
| Mortality | <2% (menstrual), 6% (nonmenstrual) | **35%** |

Clinical Presentation
- Influenza-like illness (fever, myalgias, lethargy) with diarrhea
- Diffuse erythematous rash (sunburn-like) involving palms/soles
- Progresses within **48 hours** to hypotension and multiorgan failure
- Desquamation appears after 2–3 weeks

Management
1. Hemodynamic management similar to septic shock
2. **Clindamycin** is added to inhibit toxin production
3. Source control: remove tampons/IUDs, drain abscesses, debride necrotizing infections

### ANAPHYLAXIS

Anaphylaxis is an acute multiorgan dysfunction from immunogenic release of inflammatory mediators
from basophils and mast cells. Mortality rate: **0.25–0.33%** (49).

**Table 17.4**
*Signs and Symptoms of Anaphylaxis*

| Manifestation | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Urticaria and angioedema | 60–90% |
| Upper airway angioedema | 50–60% |
| Flushing | 45–55% |
| Dyspnea, wheezing | 45–50% |
| Hypotension, syncope | 30–35% |
| Abdominal pain, diarrhea | 25–30% |
| Acute coronary syndrome | 4–5% |

Treatment

**Epinephrine** is the ONLY drug that stops anaphylaxis.

**Table 17.5**
*Aqueous Epinephrine Solutions and Their Clinical Uses*

| Dilution | Use | Dosing |
|---|---|---|
| 1:1,000 (1 mg/mL) | Anaphylaxis | 0.3–0.5 mg IM in thigh; repeat q5–15min PRN |
| 1:10,000 (0.1 mg/mL) | Asystole/PEA | 10 mL (1 mg) IV q3–5min |
| 1:100 (10 mg/mL) | Laryngeal edema | 0.25 mL (2.5 mg) nebulized |
| 1:100,000 | Anaphylactic shock | 2 mg in 200 mL saline; infuse 5–15 µg/min |

Glucagon: For refractory anaphylaxis in patients on β-blockers. Dose: **1–5 mg IV**, then 5–15 µg/min infusion.

Second-Line Agents
- **Antihistamines** (diphenhydramine 25–50 mg): symptom relief only
- **Bronchodilators** (albuterol): for bronchospasm
- **Corticosteroids**: NO role in acute management (49,50,54)

Anaphylactic Shock
- Up to **35%** of intravascular volume can be lost through leaky capillaries
- Volume resuscitation: **1–2 L crystalloid** or **500 mL 5% albumin** in first 5–10 minutes
- Consider adding colloid (albumin) since crystalloid will extravasate

### A Final Word

Inflammation (not hypoxia) as the Major Cause of Death in ICUs

1. Septic shock is the leading cause of in-hospital deaths in the US (16).
2. Organ failure in septic shock is from **mitochondrial dysfunction**, not inadequate tissue oxygenation.
3. Promoting tissue oxygenation (with O2 inhalation and RBC transfusions) may be counterproductive
because damaging effects of inflammation are largely due to oxidation.
