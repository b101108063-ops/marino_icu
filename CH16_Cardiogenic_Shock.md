## Chapter 16: Cardiogenic Shock

When is a piece of matter said to be alive? When it goes on "doing
something," moving, exchanging material with its environment.

Erwin Schrödinger (a)

The human organism goes on exchanging material with its environment by virtue of a circulatory
system equipped with an automatic stroke pump that averages about 100,000 stroke cycles daily,
and pumps a daily volume of about 8,000 liters through a network of conducting vessels that, if
placed end-to-end, would stretch more than 60,000 miles (more than twice the circumference of
the Earth!) (1). Failure of this remarkable circulatory system begins and ends with cardiac pump
failure, which can be categorized as "concerning" (conventional heart failure), "life-threatening"
(cardiogenic shock), or "catastrophic" (cardiac arrest).

This chapter focuses on the challenging condition of cardiogenic shock, where management
is designed to allow time for the heart to heal from a recent insult (e.g., acute myocardial
infarction), or for a definitive procedure to be performed (e.g., bypass surgery). The real
challenge in cardiogenic shock is that half of the patients do not survive despite the best management
efforts (2).

### 導讀摘要

- **病因**：急性心肌梗塞佔心因性休克約 2/3；需損失 ≥40% 左心室功能組織才會發生
- **心輸出量監測**：PA catheter 提供最可靠的 cardiac output、filling pressures、SVR、氧氣傳送變數；PA catheter 的使用被證實可改善預後
- **血液動力學目標**：PAWP = **19–20 mmHg**、CI ≥**2.5 L/min/m²**、MAP ≥**65 mmHg**、SvO2 >**50%**、血漿乳酸 <**2 mmol/L**
- **首選血管加壓劑**：Norepinephrine；避免使用 Dopamine（心律不整風險較高）
- **藥物治療的問題**：血管加壓劑/強心劑會增加心臟工作負擔，應及早考慮機械循環輔助（MCS）

### Pathophysiology

Cardiogenic shock can be described as cardiac pump failure that results in inadequate O2
delivery to tissues and impaired oxidative metabolism. The culmination of this condition is
multiorgan failure and a fatal outcome.

Etiologies
Acute myocardial infarction is responsible for about **two-thirds** of the cases of cardiogenic shock
(3), while the remaining cases can arise from a variety of cardiac disorders.

Acute Myocardial Infarction
Cardiogenic shock develops in **5–10%** of cases of acute myocardial infarction (3). Infarction of the
left ventricle is responsible for about 80% of the cases, and requires loss of at least **40%** of the
functional mass of the ventricle (2). Three infarct-related complications can produce cardiogenic shock:

1. **Papillary muscle rupture** (7% of cases): acute mitral insufficiency
2. **Interventricular septum rupture** (4% of cases): large left-to-right shunt
3. **Ventricular wall rupture** (1% of cases): hemopericardium and tamponade

These complications typically appear within **24 hours** of the infarction.

Other Conditions

**Table 16.1**
*Causes of Cardiogenic Shock Other than Acute MI*

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Acute decompensation | Chronic heart failure with acute decompensation (30% of cases) |
| Inflammatory | Acute myocarditis, Takotsubo's cardiomyopathy |
| Post-cardiac surgery | Postcardiotomy shock (2–5%) |
| Pregnancy-related | Peripartum cardiomyopathy |
| Mechanical | Dynamic LV outflow obstruction, Advanced valvular disease |

Hemodynamic Changes
The hemodynamic changes in cardiac pump failure include:
- **↓ Cardiac output**
- **↑ Cardiac filling pressures** (CVP or PAWP)
- **↑ Systemic vascular resistance** (compensatory vasoconstriction)

Cardiogenic shock is distinguished from heart failure by the presence of tissue hypoperfusion
(hypotension, decreased urine output) and inadequate tissue oxygenation (elevated plasma lactate).

Right Heart Failure
Cardiogenic shock from right ventricular (RV) failure has a lower mortality rate than LV failure (8).
Features of RV failure include:
- **↑ CVP/PAWP ratio**
- Dilated RV
- Normal LV ejection fraction

The Microcirculation
Studies show microcirculatory dysfunction in cardiogenic shock that is independent of global
hemodynamic measures, and has a greater impact on mortality than macrocirculatory changes (10).

Systemic Inflammation
Signs of systemic inflammation are reported in **20–40%** of patients with cardiogenic shock complicating
acute MI, with lower SVR and higher mortality rate (13).

### Management

Monitoring
PA catheters provide measurements of cardiac output, cardiac filling pressures, SVR, and oxygen
transport variables. Use of PA catheters has been shown to improve outcomes in cardiogenic
shock (14).

Goals of Management

**Table 16.2**
*Goals of Management in Cardiogenic Shock*

| Category | Goals |
|---|---|
| **Hemodynamics** | PAWP = **19–20 mmHg**, CI ≥**2.5 L/min/m²**, SVRI = **25–30 Wood Units**, Mean BP ≥**65 mmHg** |
| **Tissue Perfusion** | PCO2 Gap <**6 mmHg**, Urine output >**0.5 mL/kg/h** |
| **Tissue Oxygenation** | SvO2 >**50%**, Plasma lactate <**2 mmol/L** |

Optimizing Filling Pressures
The optimal left ventricular filling pressure (wedge pressure) is considered to be **18–20 mmHg** (17),
based on maintaining plasma colloid osmotic pressure of **20–25 mmHg**.

Vasopressor Therapy
The vasopressor of choice in cardiogenic shock is **norepinephrine** (15,18,19), a catecholamine that
produces systemic vasoconstriction and mild cardiac stimulation. Dosing: start at **5 μg/min** (or
**0.05 μg/kg/min**), titrate upward; usual dose range **5–30 μg/min** (0.05–0.5 μg/kg/min) (20).

Inotropic Therapy

**Table 16.3**
*Inotropic Agents for Cardiogenic Shock*

| Agent | Dosing | Comments |
|---|---|---|
| **Dobutamine** | 3–5 µg/kg/min initially; usual 3–20 µg/kg/min | Most widely used; increases heart rate (usually 5–15 bpm) |
| **Milrinone** | 50 µg/kg over 10 min, then 0.375–0.75 µg/kg/min | Phosphodiesterase inhibitor; risk of hypotension |
| **Levosimendan** | 12 µg/kg over 10 min, then 0.1 µg/kg/min | Calcium sensitizer; long half-life (80 hrs); not FDA approved in US |

Dobutamine
The most widely used inotropic agent (18,22). Increases β1 receptor stimulation → ↑ stroke output and
heart rate. Major disadvantage: **↑ heart rate** (usually 5–15 bpm, can exceed 30 bpm) and **↑ myocardial O2 consumption**.

Milrinone
Phosphodiesterase inhibitor → ↑ cyclic AMP → enhanced Ca²⁺ influx. Vasodilator effects can cause
hypotension. Dose adjustment needed for renal insufficiency.

Levosimendan
Sensitizes cardiac myofilaments to calcium. Dilates coronary arteries and does not stimulate myocardial
O2 consumption. Has mortality benefit over dobutamine in some studies (26).

### Mechanical Cardiac Support

Intra-Aortic Balloon Pump (IABP)
The IABP is the oldest form of MCS (introduced in 1960s). Balloon inflates during diastole
(increases coronary blood flow) and deflates during systole (decreases LV afterload). Limited by
availability and does not improve survival (28). Contraindicated in aortic valve insufficiency or
dissection.

Impella Catheters
Percutaneously inserted LV assist device. Pump moves blood from LV to proximal aorta at **2.5–5.5 L/min**.
Hemodynamic effects similar to IABP but no survival advantage over IABP in infarct-related shock (28,33).
More bleeding and limb ischemia than IABP (33,34).

ECMO
Veno-arterial ECMO (VA-ECMO) supports both circulatory flow and pulmonary gas exchange. Preferred
for cardiogenic shock with respiratory failure. Reported complications: major bleeding (**41%**),
sepsis (30%), limb ischemia (**17%**), stroke (**6%**), lower limb amputation (5%) (36).

**Major problem**: Retrograde aortic flow increases LV afterload → can worsen LV output and cause
acute pulmonary edema. Combine with IABP or Impella to reduce LV afterload.

Outcomes
Largest registry reports **42%** survival to hospital discharge with VA-ECMO (38).

Device Selection
- **VA-ECMO**: Choice for cardiogenic shock with respiratory failure
- **Impella**: Preferred over IABP in many centers
- **IABP**: Most available; only temporary measure

### A Final Word

1. Management is aimed at "buying time" until heart heals or intervention performed.
2. Pharmacological support increases cardiac workload and is counterproductive. Proceed to MCS
early if no improvement.
3. Mechanical support can "unload" ventricle (IABP), provide flow assistance (Impella), or support
gas exchange and circulation (VA-ECMO).
4. VA-ECMO is choice if cardiogenic shock accompanies respiratory failure.
5. Avoiding delays to MCS initiation optimizes outcomes.
