## Chapter 19: Tachyarrhythmias

Variability is the law of life . . .

A rapid heart rate or tachycardia while at rest is evidence of a problem, but the tachycardia itself
may not be the problem. This chapter describes tachycardias that are a problem (i.e.,
tachyarrhythmias), and require prompt evaluation and management.

### 導讀摘要

- **心房顫動（AF）心率控制**：Diltiazem（常見但有低血壓風險）、Amiodarone（可用於 HFrEF）、Metoprolol、Esmolol（短效）、Digoxin（不用於急性控制）
- **CHA₂DS₂-VASc 評分**：≥2 分（男性）或 ≥3 分（女性）建議口服抗凝；S=Stroke/TIA/VTE 是最強危險因子（2分）
- **口服抗凝藥物**：DOACs（Dabigatran、Rivaroxaban、Apixaban、Edoxaban）優先於 Warfarin
- **PSVT 急性治療**：Adenosine（6 mg → 12 mg → 12 mg 快速靜脈注射）；氣喘或AV block患者禁用
- **Torsades de Pointes**：由 QT 延長藥物引起；治療包括停藥、Magnesium、臨時起搏

### Recognition

The diagnostic evaluation of tachycardias (heart rate >100 beats/min) is based on 3 findings on
the ECG: the duration of the QRS complex, the uniformity of the R-R intervals, and the
characteristics of the atrial activity.

Narrow-QRS-Complex Tachycardias
Tachycardias with QRS ≤0.12 sec originate from above the AV conduction system (supraventricular).

**Regular Rhythm**: Possible arrhythmias include sinus tachycardia, AV nodal re-entrant tachycardia, and
atrial flutter with fixed AV block.
- Uniform P waves: Sinus tachycardia
- Absent P waves: AV nodal re-entrant tachycardia
- Sawtooth waves: Atrial flutter

**Irregular Rhythm**: Possible arrhythmias include multifocal atrial tachycardia and atrial fibrillation.
- Multiple P wave morphologies, variable PR intervals: Multifocal atrial tachycardia
- Absent P waves with disorganized atrial activity: Atrial fibrillation

Wide-QRS-Complex Tachycardias
Tachycardias with QRS >0.12 sec can originate from below the AV system (VT) or be SVT with prolonged
AV conduction.

### Atrial Fibrillation

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common cardiac arrhythmia in adults (prevalence = 2–4%).
Classified as paroxysmal (≤7 days), persistent (>7 days), long-standing persistent (>1 year), or permanent.

Hospital-Related AF
- **ICU-Related AF**: Incidence 2–44%; resolves spontaneously in up to 25% of cases
- **Postoperative AF**: Up to 45% of cardiac/thoracic surgery; up to 10% of other major surgeries

**Adverse Consequences**:
- **Cardiac Performance**: Atrial contraction is ~25% of ventricular end-diastolic volume; loss can decrease cardiac output in patients with impaired filling
- **Stroke**: AF increases stroke risk **5-fold**; risk not homogeneous—influenced by CHA₂DS₂-VASc score

**Table 19.1**
*Drug Regimens for Acute Rate Control in Atrial Fibrillation*

| Drug | Dosing Regimens and Comments |
|---|---|
| **Diltiazem** | 0.25 mg/kg IV over 2 min, then 5–15 mg/hr. Popular but limited by hypotension (20–30%) and negative inotropic effects. |
| **Amiodarone** | 150 mg IV over 10 min, repeat if needed, then 1 mg/min for 6 hr and 0.5 mg/min for 18 hr. Max 2.2 g/24 hr. Effective alternative; preferred in HFrEF. |
| **Metoprolol** | 2.5–5 mg IV over 2 min, repeat q5–10min to total of 3 doses. Most effective in hyperadrenergic states. Similar hypotension risk. |
| **Esmolol** | 500 µg/kg IV bolus, then 50 µg/kg/min; increase to max 200 µg/kg/min. Ultra short-acting (half-life 9 min). |
| **Digoxin** | 0.25 mg IV q2hr to total 1.5 mg, then 0.125–0.375 mg IV daily. Slow-acting; NOT for acute rate control alone. Used for long-term control in HFrEF. |

**Table 19.4**
*Intravenous Adenosine for Paroxysmal SVT*

| Feature | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Dosing** | 1. Deliver through peripheral vein. 2. Give 6 mg by rapid IV injection and flush with saline. 3. If no response after 2 min, give 12 mg rapid IV and flush. 4. If still inadequate after 2 min, another 12 mg can be given. |
